# Week 2 Resources

Use this page with the [Week 2 task intro](00_TASK_INTRO.md). The selected paper and the Alexine guide are the required reading. The other resources are background support: use enough of them to understand the paper, but do not treat them as extra assignments.

## Required reading

- **Paper-reading method — [Fix your paper reading game](https://alexine.rip/lab/fix-your-paper-reading-game.html).** Read this first. It gives a practical annotation sequence for moving from the problem and goal to mechanisms, evidence, limitations, and your own summary.
- **Your selected paper.** Choose one paper from the ten guides in the [task intro](00_TASK_INTRO.md), then read the paper itself rather than relying on an abstract or a secondary explanation.

## Learning ladder

Use these in order when you need a gentler route into the vocabulary behind a paper. They are accessible explainers, not additional submission requirements.

1. **[Arjun Virk — ML Guide](https://www.arjunvirk.com/writing/ml-guide).** A broad map of machine-learning ideas and terminology; use it to identify the prerequisite concepts behind a paper.
2. **[Aman AI — AI Primers](https://aman.ai/primers/ai/).** Compact primers on modern AI topics; use the relevant primer for a quick refresher before a dense section.
3. **[Alisa's Book of LLMs](https://alisawuffles.notion.site/alisa-s-book-of-llms).** A readable guide to language-model concepts; use it for tokenization, training, and LLM context.
4. **[Aleksa Gordic — Blog](https://www.aleksagordic.com/blog).** Engineering-oriented explanations of deep learning systems; use it to connect research ideas to implementation concerns.
5. **[Leerob — AI](https://leerob.com/ai).** An accessible overview of ML, neural networks, deep learning, tokenization, training, and transformers; use it to fill basic background gaps quickly.
6. **Main intuition resource — [Colah's blog](https://colah.github.io/).** Visual explanations of neural-network ideas; start here when equations or architecture diagrams feel abstract and you need intuition.

## Optional general support

- **[Dive into Deep Learning (D2L)](https://d2l.ai/).** A textbook with code, maths, and runnable explanations; use the matching chapter when you need a fuller treatment of optimization, neural networks, or attention.
- **[Stanford CS231n notes](https://cs231n.github.io/).** Clear notes on visual recognition and neural-network fundamentals; use them for layers, optimization, initialization, and convolutional-model context.
- **[Stanford CS224N](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs224n/).** Lecture material on natural-language processing and representation learning; use it for sequence models, attention, and transformer background.
- **[Understanding Deep Learning](https://udlbook.github.io/udlbook/).** A structured deep-learning textbook with visual and mathematical explanations; use it when a paper's training or generalization argument needs more depth.
- **[The Annotated Transformer](https://nlp.seas.harvard.edu/2018/04/03/attention.html).** A line-by-line implementation-oriented walkthrough of the Transformer; use it after reading the Transformer paper to connect equations to components.
- **[Let's build GPT from scratch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kCc8FmEb1nY).** A hands-on video that builds a small language model; use it for an optional concrete bridge from attention concepts to code.

## Optional embedded and TinyML supplements

Use a supplement only to clarify the selected paper's setting, terminology, or deployment path. Supplements do not replace the selected paper or the mandatory paper-reading guide.

- **[LiteRT for Microcontrollers overview](https://ai.google.dev/edge/litert/microcontrollers/overview).** Current Google AI Edge documentation for deploying inference on microcontrollers; use it to understand the runtime context around the TensorFlow Lite Micro paper.
- **[TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers documentation](https://www.tensorflow.org/lite/microcontrollers).** The original TensorFlow documentation for the microcontroller workflow; use it for terminology and practical constraints.
- **[MLPerf Tiny benchmark](https://mlcommons.org/working-groups/benchmarks/tiny/).** The benchmark landing page and its [reference repository](https://github.com/mlcommons/tiny) describe workloads, rules, and implementations; use them to decode the MLPerf Tiny paper's evaluation setting.
- **[Digi-Key TinyML video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gDFWCxrJruQ).** An accessible walkthrough of TinyML with TensorFlow Lite for Microcontrollers; use it for a quick deployment-oriented overview.
- **[TinyTorch getting started](https://mlsysbook.ai/tinytorch/getting-started.html).** A small implementation practice path for learning deep-learning building blocks; use it only when a paper's mechanics need a hands-on mental model.
- **[MLSysBook](https://www.mlsysbook.ai/).** A broader guide to machine-learning systems and their deployment trade-offs; use it for system-level context around memory, latency, and hardware constraints.
- **[MIT HAN Lab MCUNet lecture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YBER-SNlkqs).** An optional advanced talk about MCUNet; use it for extra intuition on tiny neural-network design and deployment.
