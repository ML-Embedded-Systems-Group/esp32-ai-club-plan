## Phase 2: Coding, PPL_FROM_LOSS.py, the converter (about 2 hours)

The club converts every eval run with this script. Build it now:

1. Write a script, under 35 lines, stdlib only (math, sys).
2. The script accepts arguments: one loss value, or none.
3. With a loss: print ppl = e to the power of the loss, rounded to 2 decimals, and the average per-word probability: 1 divided by ppl.
4. With no argument: print the reverse direction for the repo's reported ppl 12.58: the loss = math.log(12.58), and 1 divided by 12.58.
5. With more than one loss value given: process each, print one line per value.
6. Run it three ways: one loss of your choice, no argument, and three losses of your choice. Keep all outputs.

Save it as PPL_FROM_LOSS.py. Write the script yourself. Do not paste from any AI tool.

## Phase 3: Maths, perplexity by hand and derived (about 2-3 hours)

Show every step, by hand, no calculator shortcuts:

1. Set A: cat 0.5, dog 0.3, sun 0.2. Sequence: cat, sun, dog, cat. Multiply the 4 probabilities step by step.
2. Set A: ppl = product to the power -1/4. Show the power step and the result, rounded to 2 decimals.
3. Set B: cat 0.4, dog 0.35, sun 0.25. Same sequence. Repeat steps 1 and 2.
4. Derivation: rewrite ppl as the fourth root of the product of the inverse probabilities: (1/p1 times 1/p2 times 1/p3 times 1/p4) to the power 1/4. Verify it gives the same result as step 2.
5. A uniform distribution over 3 words has ppl 3.0. Which of your two sets is closer to 3.0? Write 2 sentences: what makes ppl go down, and what ppl measures.
6. One sentence: the results page reports ppl 12.58. What does that number mean for the average word?
