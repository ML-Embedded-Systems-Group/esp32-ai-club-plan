## Phase 2: Maths, PWM by hand (about 2 hours)

Show every step:

1. Frequency 5000 Hz: period = 1 divided by 5000, in microseconds. Show the division.
2. Duty 50%: on time = 0.5 times the period. Compute.
3. Duty 25%: on time = 0.25 times the period. Compute.
4. Resolution: 2 to the power 8 = 256 levels. 2 to the power 10 = 1024. 2 to the power 14 = 16384. Write all three.
5. One level at 8 bits: 1 divided by 256, times 100, as a percentage.
6. On time for duty values 64, 128, 192 at 8 bits and 5000 Hz: duty = value divided by 256, times the period. Compute all three.
7. Write 2 sentences: what the resolution limits, and why 14 bits is smoother than 8.

## Phase 3: Coding, PWM_REPRO.py (about 2-3 hours)

Reproduce the published resolution math:

1. Write a script, under 40 lines, stdlib only.
2. Given a frequency in Hz and a resolution in bits: print the period in us, the number of levels (2 to the power bits), and the on time for duty values at 25%, 50%, 75%.
3. Reproduce the docs' resolution range: print the level count for 8, 10, 14 bits.
4. Verify: 2 to the power 14 = 16384. Print match or differ.
5. Print the on-time table for 5000 Hz at 8 bits for duty values 64, 128, 192. These must match your phase 2 numbers.
6. Print the finest on-time step at 14 bits and 5000 Hz: period divided by 16384, in ns.
