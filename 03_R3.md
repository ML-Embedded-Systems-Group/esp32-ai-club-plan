## Phase 2: Coding, BPE_ENCODE.py, the tokenizer (about 2-3 hours)

The chip runs this same algorithm. Build a working encoder and decoder:

1. Download the real GPT-2 tokenizer files:
   - https://huggingface.co/gpt2/resolve/main/merges.txt
   - https://huggingface.co/gpt2/resolve/main/vocab.json
   Save them in your working folder.
2. Write a script, under 90 lines, stdlib only.
3. Load merges.txt (the merge rules) and vocab.json (piece to id, and id to piece).
4. encode(text): split the text into characters and spaces. Apply the merge rules in order: for each rule, scan the token list and merge every adjacent pair that matches the rule. This is a real BPE encoder, slow but correct.
5. decode(ids): reverse: join the pieces from vocab.json, with the space handling from the merges format (a piece starting with a space gets a space before it).
6. The script takes a text argument. It prints: the pieces, their ids, the token count, and the decoded round trip.
7. Run it on 5 sentences of your choice. Print the counts. Verify for each: decode(encode(text)) equals the original text.

Save it as BPE_ENCODE.py. Write the script yourself. Do not paste from any AI tool.

## Phase 3: Maths, vocab bits (about 2 hours)

Show every step, with units:

1. GPT-2 vocab is 50257. Compute log2(50257) by hand: find the power of 2 that is larger: 2 to the power 15 is 32768, 2 to the power 16 is 65536. How many bits per index?
2. LLaMA vocab is 32000. Which power covers it? How many bits?
3. The repo vocab is 32768. Which power is it exactly?
4. Bytes per index at 15 bits and at 16 bits: divide by 8.
5. One million tokens: total bits at 15 bits, then in MB (divide by 8, then by 1,000,000). Same for 16 bits. Difference in MB.
6. Savings from 16 to 15 bits as a percentage: difference divided by the 16-bit size, times 100.
7. Write 2 sentences: what a bigger vocab gives, and what it costs.
