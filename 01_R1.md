## Phase 2: Maths, entropy by hand (about 2 hours)

Show every step:

1. Shannon counts 27 symbols: 26 letters plus space. Compute log2(27) by hand: find the exponent x where 2 to the power x equals 27. Show the bounds: 2 to the power 4 is 16, 2 to the power 5 is 32. What is x?
2. Verify: log2(27) = 4.755 rounds to 4.76. This is the article's zeroth-order value. Write why a uniform model has this entropy.
3. Take a 4-symbol alphabet with probabilities 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.125. Compute H = -sum of p times log2(p) for all 4, step by step.
4. Write 2 sentences: why entropy falls from 4.76 to 4.03 when you use letter frequencies, and what the drop means.

## Phase 3: Coding, ENTROPY_EN.py (about 2-3 hours)

Reproduce the article's two numbers on a real text:

1. Download an English text: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/11/pg11.txt (Alice in Wonderland). Save it as alice.txt.
2. Write a script, under 60 lines, stdlib only (math, collections).
3. Read alice.txt, keep only letters a-z and space. Count every symbol.
4. Zeroth-order entropy: log2 of the number of distinct symbols. Print it.
5. First-order entropy: -sum of (count divided by total) times log2 of (count divided by total). Print it, rounded to 3 decimals.
6. Compare: print the article's values 4.76 and 4.03, and the difference for each.
7. The first-order value on a long text lands near 4.0. Print whether your value is within 0.3 of 4.03.

Save it as ENTROPY_EN.py. Write the script yourself. Do not paste from any AI tool.
