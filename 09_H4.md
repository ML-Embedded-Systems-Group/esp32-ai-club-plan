## Phase 2: Coding, TIMING_CALC.py, the timing calculator (about 1-2 hours)

The club uses this to convert measured reads into tok/s, on any machine or chip. Build it now:

1. Write a script, under 25 lines, stdlib only (math).
2. It takes the average read time in microseconds as an argument, plus the number of rows per token, default 6.
3. It prints: read time per token in us (rows times the read time), per token in ms, the tok/s (1,000,000 divided by us per token), and the row-read share of one token time (rows times read time divided by token time, times 100).
4. Run it with 20.3 us and 6 rows (the chip numbers). Keep the output.
5. Run it with your own measured read time from H1's FLOW_BENCH.py if you have it. Keep the output.

Save it as TIMING_CALC.py. Write the script yourself. Do not paste from any AI tool.

## Phase 3: Maths, timing (about 1 hour)

Show every step:

1. One token at 9.88 tok/s: 1 divided by 9.88, in seconds. Convert to milliseconds.
2. Row reads: 6 rows times 20.3 us. Convert to milliseconds.
3. Share of one token: row reads divided by the token time, times 100. Round to 2 decimals. Is it a large or small share?
4. Two sentences: what dominates the token time, and why row reads matter.
