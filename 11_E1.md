## Phase 2: Maths, clock by hand (about 2 hours)

Show every step:

1. Clock 240 MHz: cycles per second = 240,000,000.
2. Cycle time: 1 divided by 240,000,000, in nanoseconds. Show the division.
3. Verify: 1/240 MHz = 4.1667 ns. Round to 4 decimals.
4. A delay of 1000 ms: cycles = 240,000,000 times 1. Compute.
5. If one loop iteration takes 10 cycles: iterations = cycles divided by 10. Compute.
6. A delay of 200 ms: cycles and iterations. Same steps.
7. Write 2 sentences: what a clock cycle is, and why a slower clock means a longer delay.

## Phase 3: Coding, CLOCK_REPRO.py (about 2-3 hours)

Reproduce the published clock math, and measure a real loop:

1. Write a script, under 40 lines, stdlib only (time).
2. Reproduce the docs' numbers: given a clock in MHz, print cycles per second, cycle time in ns, cycles in a delay in ms, and loop iterations at a cost of 10 cycles per iteration.
3. Print the row for 240 MHz and delays 1000 ms and 200 ms. These must match your phase 2 numbers.
4. Measure a real loop: time 1,000,000 empty loop iterations with time.perf_counter. Print the ns per iteration on this machine.
5. Print the comparison: your machine's loop cost vs the 10 cycles at 240 MHz assumption.

Save it as CLOCK_REPRO.py. Write the script yourself. Do not paste from any AI tool.
